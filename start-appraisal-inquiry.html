What to prepare before reaching out

For a direct inquiry, email Tony Zhou at tonyzhouacademy@gmail.com. Please include the intended use of the appraisal and the basic object information below.

  • Clear photographs of the whole object, details, signatures, seals, inscriptions, labels, and condition issues.
  • Dimensions, material, medium, mounting, frame, edition, or object category if known.
  • Provenance, purchase records, invoices, prior appraisals, exhibition records, publication history, or family notes.
  • The reason the value is needed: estate, donation, insurance, family division, sale planning, or collection review.
  • The relevant date or timing requirement, especially for estate, donation, insurance renewal, or legal context.

Chinese and Asian art inquiries

For Chinese and Asian art, include images of inscriptions, seals, mounts, bases, marks, labels, boxes, receipts, and any translation or attribution notes. Cross-cultural valuation often depends on object category, period language, condition vocabulary, provenance, and the market in which comparable evidence is selected.

What happens next

  1. Clarify the intended use and whether FMV, replacement value, or another value basis is needed.
  2. Review whether the object or collection requires identification, triage, or a formal appraisal scope.
  3. Separate appraisal questions from tax, legal, insurance coverage, or sale-advisory questions that require other professionals.
  4. Move the assignment into the appropriate documentation path when the scope is clear.

Report format and final fees are confirmed after scope review. See Art appraisal fees and report types for how Restricted Appraisal Reports, Appraisal Reports, and complex assignments are separated.

This page is educational and does not provide tax, legal, estate, or insurance coverage advice. Coordinate appraisal use with the relevant attorney, tax advisor, insurer, or estate professional.
