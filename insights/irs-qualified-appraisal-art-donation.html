Insight

IRS qualified appraisal for donated art: prepare the evidence before the number.

A strong donation appraisal starts with object identity, use case, timing, and market support. The valuation conclusion comes after the evidence is organized.

Official resources first

Before making tax decisions, review IRS Publication 561, Form 8283, and the IRS Art Appraisal Services page with your tax advisor.

What to gather

  • Object photographs, dimensions, medium, inscriptions, marks, and labels.
  • Acquisition record, invoice, provenance notes, prior appraisals, and insurance schedules.
  • Condition, restoration, frame, mount, or conservation information.
  • Donee organization, intended donation date, and any advisor instructions.

Why Asian art needs extra preparation

A Chinese porcelain, Japanese print, or South Asian sculpture may require category correction before market comparables can be selected. Translation, attribution language, and condition terminology can materially change the valuation file.
