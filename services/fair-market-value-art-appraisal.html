Core service

Fair market value art appraisal for decisions that need defensible evidence.

FMV is not a casual estimate. It is a value conclusion built from object identification, condition, provenance, effective date, market comparables, and the reason the appraisal is being used.

When FMV is the right starting point

Fair market value is commonly requested for art donation appraisal, estate art valuation, family division, financial reporting, and other market-based questions. It may not be the same value basis used for insurance replacement.

What the appraisal file needs

  • Clear photographs of the object, marks, signatures, inscriptions, labels, and damage.
  • Measurements, materials, medium, mounting, frame, edition, and known alterations.
  • Provenance, purchase records, invoices, prior appraisals, exhibition history, and publication history.
  • The intended use of the appraisal and the effective date of value.
  • Comparable sales and market context appropriate to the object category.

Where Chinese and Asian art complicate FMV

For Chinese and Asian art, valuation often turns on translation between market languages. A porcelain form, bronze inscription, painting attribution, seal, mounting style, condition term, or export history can change how comparables are selected. See the topic page on Chinese and Asian art valuation.
