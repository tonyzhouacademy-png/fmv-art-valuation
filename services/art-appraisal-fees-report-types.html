Services and fees

Art appraisal cost depends on the assignment, not on a hierarchy of accuracy.

Every appraisal conclusion must be credible for its intended use. Fee differences usually come from the level of report disclosure, research complexity, intended users, documentation burden, and the risk of third-party review.

By Tony Zhou ISA Accredited Member (AM) Art Appraiser | Chinese & Asian Art | Cross-cultural Valuation

Starting fees

Report format follows intended use.

Single-object assignments generally begin at US$150 per item for a Restricted Appraisal Report and US$300 per item for an Appraisal Report. The appropriate format is determined after the intended use and intended users are identified.

Generally from $150 per item

Restricted Appraisal Report

Appropriate when the client and intended use are clearly identified and a concise reporting format is suitable. A restricted report is not a casual estimate or a lower-credibility value conclusion; "restricted" primarily describes how the analysis is communicated.

Generally from $300 per item

Appraisal Report

Appropriate when the assignment requires fuller explanation of the object, intended use, effective date, value basis, scope of work, relevant market, comparables, analysis, assumptions, and limiting conditions.

Scope review

Complex or special-purpose assignments

Tax, institutional review, contested, legal, or cross-market assignments are quoted after scope review. Collection assignments may also be quoted as an overall project when multiple objects share a coordinated research path.

Fee independence

Appraisal fees are never calculated as a percentage of the appraised value and are not contingent on reaching a particular value conclusion or financial outcome. Consistent with USPAP and the ISA Code of Ethics, fees are based instead on the scope of work, research and reporting requirements, assignment complexity, intended use, and relevant market conditions.

A higher-value object may require more extensive research, documentation, or review, but any difference in fee must arise from the work and responsibility involved—not from the appraised value itself.

Fees may increase because the assignment is more complex, but never because the final value conclusion itself is higher. This keeps the appraisal process independent from the number reported in the conclusion.

For professional context, see the ISA Code of Ethics and USPAP information from The Appraisal Foundation.

Why a simple price table can be misleading

A lower-value work may still require extensive research if authorship, condition, provenance, or market identity is unclear. A higher-value object with complete documentation and a well-established market may sometimes be more direct. The relevant question is not only "what is it worth?" but what work is required to support the value conclusion for the intended use.

When scope review changes the fee

  • Tax, legal, institutional, or contested use.
  • Market evidence that cannot be exchanged casually between mainland China, Hong Kong, the United States, Europe, or another relevant market.
  • Scarce comparable sales or comparables that require careful adjustment.
  • Attribution, provenance, condition, or authenticity-related caveats.
  • Coordination with lawyers, tax advisors, insurers, institutions, or estate executors.
  • Special assignment conditions, urgent deadlines, or large volumes of supporting documents.

Chinese and Asian art market scope

Chinese and Asian art assignments often require a careful definition of the relevant market. Hong Kong, mainland China, European, and U.S. markets may involve different buyer structures, legal conditions, attribution language, circulation histories, and levels of information availability. Those markets cannot be merged into a simple price list without reviewing the object category, evidence, intended use, and effective date.

Per-item starting fees are not fixed quotes for every object or collection. Final fees are confirmed only after the intended use, intended users, effective date, object category, available materials, deadline, and scope of work are reviewed.
