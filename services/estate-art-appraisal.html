Estate valuation

Estate art appraisal for inherited collections and date-specific FMV.

Estate valuation is often less about one object and more about a family, a date, a record trail, and a collection that needs to become administratively legible.

When an estate art appraisal is needed

Estate art appraisal can support estate administration, family division, date-of-death valuation, collection triage, sale planning, charitable transfer planning, and insurance review during transition.

What makes estate valuation different

  • The effective date may be fixed by estate needs rather than the inspection date.
  • Families may need both individual object values and collection-level priority.
  • Objects may have incomplete records, unclear attribution, or inherited verbal provenance.
  • Some works need market comparables; others first need identification and category correction.

Chinese and Asian art estate issues

Inherited Asian art collections often include mixed categories: porcelain, scrolls, furniture, bronzes, jade, textiles, prints, religious objects, and decorative works. The first job is to separate what is culturally interesting from what is market-significant, then decide which items deserve deeper FMV work.

Recommended path

Begin with a collection inventory, then move high-priority objects into individual FMV files. If the estate includes Chinese or Asian art, read the Chinese and Asian art valuation guide and the Asian art estate division case study.

This page is educational and does not provide estate, tax, or legal advice. Coordinate the appraisal scope with the estate attorney or administrator.
