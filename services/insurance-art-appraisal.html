Insurance appraisal

Insurance art appraisal starts by naming the value basis.

Insurance questions often require replacement value, not fair market value. The appraisal file should make that distinction visible before numbers are compared.

FMV and replacement value are different tools

Fair market value asks one kind of market question. Insurance replacement often asks what it would cost to replace a work with a comparable object in the relevant retail or specialist market. The two numbers can diverge, especially for rare Asian art categories with uneven supply.

Insurance appraisal materials

  • High-resolution images, including condition and identifying marks.
  • Current location, ownership, purchase records, and prior insurance schedules.
  • Condition notes, restoration records, frame or mounting information, and conservation reports.
  • Coverage purpose: new schedule, renewal, collection review, or claim support.

Chinese and Asian art coverage risks

Translation errors, vague category descriptions, missing dimensions, and old retail values can create coverage friction. A stronger file uses precise object language and a value basis that matches the insurance purpose.
