Donation appraisal

Art donation appraisal for IRS fair market value questions.

Donation appraisals need a careful value premise, clear object evidence, market comparables, and timing discipline. The goal is not a flattering number; it is a supportable FMV conclusion.

What this pathway is for

This page is for donors, collectors, family offices, museums, universities, and advisors asking how to approach an art donation appraisal. It is especially relevant for long-tail questions such as art donation appraisal IRS, qualified appraisal for donated art, and Chinese art donation fair market value.

IRS boundaries to know

The IRS publishes Publication 561 for determining the value of donated property, Form 8283 for noncash charitable contributions, and information about Art Appraisal Services. Use those official resources and your tax advisor to confirm filing duties and thresholds.

Common donation appraisal problems

  • The donor has auction estimates but no full condition or comparable analysis.
  • The object category is described differently in English, Chinese, and auction catalog language.
  • The prior purchase price is old, private, incomplete, or from a different market cycle.
  • The donation includes multiple works with different liquidity profiles.
  • The intended donee needs a clear record of what is being received.

Chinese and Asian art donation issues

For Chinese ceramics, paintings, bronzes, Buddhist objects, scholar's objects, Japanese prints, Korean ceramics, South Asian sculpture, and Southeast Asian works, the appraisal must translate cultural knowledge into market evidence. A title translation alone is not enough.
